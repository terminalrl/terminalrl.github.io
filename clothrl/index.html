Robot manipulation

clothrl

A simulated Franka Panda arm learns to pick up a tapered cork, carry it and press it into a jar, to stack cubes, and to drape a cloth over the jar. Every policy is trained with reinforcement learning from a sparse reward: a bonus when the task is done, and nothing that says “get closer”. The long-term goal is cloth-mediated insertion, pressing the cork into the jar through a cloth draped over its mouth so the cloth is trapped, the soft counterpart of gasket, membrane and seal assembly.

Making a sparse reward work

In typical RL approaches, dense rewards give the policy a signal at every step about what it's doing right or wrong. However, this can often result in suboptimal policies, or lots of developer time spent tuning reward functions and coefficients. Sparse rewards avoid that, but they're hard to learn from.

  • Start close to success. To kickstart the critic, early training mostly uses initial states that are already close to success. Once the policy can complete the simple versions and values winning states, it slowly learns the full task space. I use an adaptive sampler to choose the initial states that will help the policy learn the most given its current progress: it doesn't sample states it has already learned, and it doesn't sample states that are still too hard.
  • Design the starts and the success rule carefully. For this to work, the possible initial states have to be designed so that each one is actually feasible, and “success” has to be well defined. For the cork, I first defined success as simply being inserted in the jar. That led to two issues: the cork would sometimes end up stable but inserted at an angle, and the policy learned to just drop the cork once it was close rather than insert it (from the initial states right above the jar, the simplest action is to let go). That's technically success, and could be a valid strategy, but it likely won't give a very stable policy, especially once sim-to-real transfer is considered. So I required the hand to have touched the cork while it was seated, as a gate before success. Later I revisited this: with better initial-state design (so the policy learns that placing carefully works more often), it learned to place the cork without dropping it about 3x faster without the gate than with it.
  • Other things that helped: rewriting Isaac Lab's relative joint position action so that it holds one target per policy step rather than re-applying the offset at every physics step (relevant with decimation); a lot of iteration on the Newton physics and VBD solver parameters for the cork and the cloth; and adjusting the robot arm's PD gains.

Cloth

Before any learning, a standalone cloth simulation tested the core idea: if a cork is pressed into the jar through a cloth, is the cloth actually trapped? With the cork seated, all of the cloth in the bore stays when the cloth edge is pulled; with no cork, none of it does.

The drape policy above pinches a sheet standing in a slot and throws it over the jar, with the same sparse reward. It used a stiffer cloth than the proof of concept. Work in progress: a soft, tablecloth-like cloth lying on the table with nothing holding it, picked up by the edge that hangs over the table, then the full drape-and-insert task.

Setup

RobotFranka Panda, 7-DoF arm with a parallel gripper, realistic joint gains and velocity limits
PhysicsIsaac Lab on the Newton engine (MuJoCo-Warp for rigid bodies, VBD for cloth), GPU-parallel
LearningPPO, 1,024 parallel environments on one GPU, 30 Hz control
Actionbounded joint-position steps (up to 2.5 rad/s) and an open/close gripper
Reward+2 once on success; a negligible penalty on failure; tiny action and joint-speed penalties. No distance shaping.

Trained policies are not published. All results are from simulation.
