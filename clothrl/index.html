Robot manipulation

clothrl

A simulated Franka Panda arm learns to pick up a tapered cork, carry it and press it into a jar, to stack cubes, and to drape a cloth over the jar. Every policy is trained with reinforcement learning from a sparse reward: a bonus when the task is done, and nothing that says “get closer”. The long-term goal is cloth-mediated insertion, pressing the cork into the jar through a cloth draped over its mouth so the cloth is trapped, the soft counterpart of gasket, membrane and seal assembly.

Making a sparse reward work

With only a success bonus, a random arm essentially never finishes the task, so the policy gets no learning signal at all. Most of the work was making that signal reachable, and removing bugs that silently made the task impossible.

  • Start near the goal, then move the start back. Each episode begins in one of about fifteen start states laid backwards along the task: cork already seated, about to insert, hovering, carried, just grasped, hand near the cork, the full task. An adaptive sampler spends episodes where the success rate of reaching the next stage is near 50%, so the policy always practises its frontier and success propagates backwards one stage at a time.
  • Check outcomes, not intentions. The first “grasped” starts were never grasped: the reset computed the hand pose and never applied it, so the cork fell through the air. The reset now measures the real cork-to-fingertip distance every time.
  • Define success so it can't be gamed, but don't over-constrain it. Loose rules were exploited (a cork dropped from above self-centres in the jar). Yet the strictest rule, requiring the hand on the cork as it seats, slowed learning about threefold. Letting a dropped-and-seated cork count, and letting the success rate itself teach that careful placement is more reliable, reached 99% on the full task in a third of the training.
  • Evaluate the deterministic policy. Training numbers come from the noisy exploration policy. Several times they disagreed with the real policy by a wide margin, and each gap turned out to be a real bug.
  • Get the physics right. Realistic arm gains and joint-velocity limits, resets that write every joint's position and velocity (an incompletely reset simulation re-exploded every step), and a contact model where a pinched cube does not spin in the grip.
  • A bounded action. One clamped joint step per control tick, instead of a relative command re-applied every physics sub-step, which behaved like an unbounded velocity command.

Cloth

Before any learning, a standalone cloth simulation tested the core idea: if a cork is pressed into the jar through a cloth, is the cloth actually trapped? With the cork seated, all of the cloth in the bore stays when the cloth edge is pulled; with no cork, none of it does. The seal held across an eightfold range of cork-to-bore gap.

The drape policy above pinches a sheet standing in a slot and throws it over the jar, with the same sparse reward. It used a stiffer cloth than the proof of concept. Work in progress: a soft, tablecloth-like cloth lying on the table with nothing holding it, picked up by the edge that hangs over the table, then the full drape-and-insert task.

Setup

RobotFranka Panda, 7-DoF arm with a parallel gripper, realistic joint gains and velocity limits
PhysicsIsaac Lab on the Newton engine (MuJoCo-Warp for rigid bodies, VBD for cloth), GPU-parallel
LearningPPO, 1,024 parallel environments on one GPU, 30 Hz control
Actionbounded joint-position steps (up to 2.5 rad/s) and an open/close gripper
Reward+2 once on success; a negligible penalty on failure; tiny action and joint-speed penalties. No distance shaping.

Trained policies are not published. All results are from simulation.
